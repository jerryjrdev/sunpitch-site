Accuracy and calibration

How accurate is the pitch gauge?

On a smooth surface, a flip-averaged reading is within about ±0.3°. Directly on shingles, granules and uneven decking add about ±1°. For the closer figure, lay a level or a straightedge on the roof and set the phone on that, or set the phone on its edge on a rafter.

The tolerance for each reading is shown next to it on the Gauge. The methodology page explains how every number is produced.

What is Flip-average, and when should I use it?

Every phone, and every case, sits a little off true. Flip-average cancels that offset:

  1. Set the phone down and hold still until the reading settles.
  2. Turn it end-for-end on the same spot.
  3. Hold still again. SunPitch averages the two readings.

Use it whenever the number matters, for example for a bid or for cutting rafters, and whenever you change phone cases.

What does Zero do?

Zero stores the phone's offset on a surface you trust to be level, such as a checked level or a machined table, and subtracts it from every reading. Set it on a surface that is truly level, or the offset makes readings worse. Reset zero in the calibration sheet if you're unsure. Flip-average doesn't need a level surface at all.

How good is the camera pitch estimate?

Camera mode measures the angle of a roof edge seen edge-on, such as a gable rake or the end of a hip roof. SunPitch removes the phone's own tilt using gravity, so you don't need to hold the phone perfectly upright.

It's always an estimate. For the best result, stand square-on to the edge, keep the edge away from the corners of the frame and place the handles carefully. The level bar warns you when the phone is tilted more than 35°. How close the estimate gets depends mostly on the viewing angle.

Why does the sun path need calibrating?

Phone compasses often read 5–15° off near metal roofs, racking, vehicles and steel framing. A sun path drawn on a compass heading that far off would be misleading, so SunPitch asks you to calibrate the heading before it draws one. After calibration, the gyroscope carries the heading, not the compass.

Sun sight or eave sight: which should I use?

Sun sight is about ±1°. Use it when the sun is visible: hold the crosshair on the sun for about 2 seconds. Look at the screen, never at the sun.

Eave sight is about ±2–4°. Use it when the sun is hidden: tap both ends of an eave on the satellite map, then stand at the first corner and aim along the real eave. Satellite imagery can be slightly offset, so if the sun comes out, a sun sight is the closer check.

Headings drift over time. SunPitch asks you to re-sight after 3 minutes on the sun path and after 90 seconds in Align Panel.

What does “Magnetic disturbance” mean?

SunPitch compares the magnetic field the phone measures with the value the World Magnetic Model expects at your location. When they differ by more than 15%, something nearby, usually metal, is throwing the compass off. SunPitch keeps using your calibrated heading and ignores the compass. Recalibrate a few steps away from metal if you can.

Can I use the sun path as a production or shade report?

No. Sun paths are estimates and a visual guide for planning a site. They are not a production, savings or financing report, and they don't replace a professional shade survey.

Subscriptions

What does SunPitch cost?

SunPitch requires a SunPitch Pro subscription, which unlocks every feature. In the United States it costs $13.99 per week or $129.99 per year. The App Store shows the price in your currency before you subscribe.

How does the free trial work?

Both plans start with a 1-week free trial for new subscribers, one trial per Apple Account. You're charged when the trial ends unless you cancel at least 24 hours before it ends.

How do I cancel?
  1. On your iPhone, open Settings and tap your name.
  2. Tap Subscriptions, then SunPitch.
  3. Tap Cancel Subscription.

You can also open Settings in SunPitch and tap Manage subscription. Cancel at least 24 hours before the trial or the current period ends to avoid the next charge. You keep access until the end of the period you've paid for.

How do I restore my purchase on a new iPhone?

Sign in to the App Store with the Apple Account you subscribed with, open SunPitch and tap Restore purchases. It's on the subscription screen and in Settings. Restoring doesn't charge you again.

Projects aren't moved by Restore purchases: they live on the device they were made on. Use an iPhone backup to move them to a new phone.

Can I get a refund?

Apple handles all payments and refunds, and we can't issue them ourselves. Request a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com: sign in, choose the SunPitch subscription and tell Apple what went wrong.

Troubleshooting

The camera shows “Camera access is off”.

Open your iPhone's Settings, scroll to SunPitch and turn on Camera. Camera mode, sun sight, eave sight and the sun path need it. Camera frames are processed on your iPhone and never uploaded.

The sun path or the map can't find my location.

Open Settings, then SunPitch, then Location, and choose While Using the App. Keep Precise Location on so the map and the sun's position are right for your roof. Location is used on your iPhone and never sent to us.

Also check that Date & Time is set automatically (Settings, General, Date & Time). A wrong time zone moves the sun.

Save Image doesn't save to Photos.

Open Settings, then SunPitch, then Photos, and choose Add Photos Only. SunPitch only adds images and can't see the rest of your library.

My sun sight was rejected.

SunPitch rejects a sight when the bright spot is too far from where the sun must be. Check that your location and time zone are set automatically, aim away from reflections in windows, metal and water, and try again. If the sun is behind cloud, use an eave sight instead.

Readings jump around on the Gauge.

Set the phone on something firm and hold still for a second; Hold locks the reading once it settles. Wind, a flexing straightedge or loose granules make the reading wander. If it reads “Tilt sensor unavailable”, restart SunPitch, or use Camera mode for an estimate.

Satellite imagery isn't available for my address.

Apple Maps doesn't have imagery everywhere. Use a sun sight if the sun is out, or type the face's bearing in by hand. SunPitch marks a typed bearing as manual.

Where are my projects stored?

On your iPhone, inside the app. SunPitch 1.0 doesn't sync between devices, and deleting the app deletes its projects. Export a PDF report to keep a copy elsewhere, and keep iPhone backups turned on.
