Pitch on a surface

SunPitch reads the phone's motion sensors and measures the angle of whatever the phone lies on: a rafter, a straightedge or the roof surface.

Hold averages one second of steady readings. Flip-average takes a reading, turns the phone end-for-end on the same spot and averages the two, which cancels the phone's own sensor offset. On a smooth surface a flip-averaged reading is within about ±0.3°.

Shingle granules and uneven decking add about ±1°. Lay a level or a straightedge under the phone for the closer figure.

Camera estimate

Camera mode estimates pitch from a roof edge seen edge-on, such as a gable rake or the end of a hip roof. SunPitch removes the phone's own tilt using gravity and the camera's lens data, then measures the line you place.

Results are always an estimate. The edge must be seen square-on; viewing it at an angle, lens distortion near the frame edges and handle placement all move the result.

Heading calibration

Phone compasses drift and swing near metal roofs, racking and vehicles, so SunPitch does not rely on the raw compass for face direction.

A sun sight compares where the camera sees the sun with where the sun must be at this time and place: about ±1°. An eave sight lines the camera up with an eave you mark on the map: about ±2–4°, depending on how precisely the eave is marked.

The World Magnetic Model (WMM2025) converts compass readings to true north and flags magnetic disturbance. It is a check on the compass, not a replacement for calibration.

Sun position

Sun paths use NOAA's solar position equations, based on Jean Meeus's Astronomical Algorithms, which place the sun within about 0.05° for present-day dates. A sun path is only as accurate as the heading calibration under it.

Roof numbers

Pitch is shown three ways from the same angle θ: rise per 12 of run is 12 × tan θ, percent is 100 × tan θ, and degrees is θ itself. The Gauge snaps the large number to the nearest whole x/12 and always shows the exact value beside it.

  • Pitch factor is the sloped length divided by the plan length: 1 ÷ cos θ, or √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²). A 6/12 roof has a pitch factor of 1.118.
  • Hip/valley factor is the hip or valley length divided by the common run: √(rise² + 288) ÷ 12. A 6/12 roof has a hip/valley factor of 1.500.
  • Squares are true roof area divided by 100 sq ft, after the waste allowance you choose.

Estimates for planning

Sun paths, panel angles and camera results are estimates for planning.

They are not a production report, a bankable energy figure or a substitute for a professional survey.
