Roof pitch gauge

Lay your iPhone on a rafter, a straightedge or the roof surface, or stand it on its long edge, and read pitch live in x/12, degrees and percent. Pitch factor and hip/valley factor update with every reading.

  • Hold locks a steady reading, with a haptic and an optional spoken readout, so your eyes stay on your footing.
  • Average takes three readings. Flip-average reads, turns the phone end-for-end and reads again, cancelling case and camera-bump error.
  • Zero sets the offset on a surface you trust to be level.

Camera pitch estimate

Line up a roof edge seen edge-on in the live camera, such as a gable rake or the end of a hip roof. SunPitch removes the phone's own tilt using gravity and gives a quick pitch estimate. Every camera result is labeled as an estimate.

±1°Heading by sun sight

Sun path over your roof

Phone compasses read 5–15° off near metal roofs and racking, so SunPitch never relies on the compass alone. Calibrate the heading once:

  • Sun sight: hold the crosshair on the sun for about 2 seconds. About ±1°. Look at the screen, never at the sun.
  • Eave sight: trace the eave on a satellite map, then sight along the real one. About ±2–4°, and it works when the sun is hidden.

Then see the sun's path in the camera for today, both solstices, the equinox or any date, with hour marks. Pick a roof face, and the hours when the sun is behind it are dashed.

Work safe

Placing the phone on a roof is for trained roofers using fall protection. Set it down only when you are tied off and stable, and never use SunPitch while walking on a roof.

Homeowners: stay off the roof. Use the camera estimate from a safe spot, or measure a rafter in the attic.

When sighting the sun, look at the screen, never at the sun.
